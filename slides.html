ACIO

Augmented Investment Intelligence

An AI investment committee for pensions, endowments and foundations that hire external managers.

Bryan White · MGT 409 · Yale SOM · November 2026

Live app: https://acio.onrender.com/ · Code: https://github.com/bjw49/Bryan-White-Final-Project

The problem

  • Investment teams, small and large, at pensions, endowments, foundations and insurers that hire external managers
  • Two linked decisions take most of their time: the asset allocation and which managers to hire, and in what size
  • The manager universe is huge (27,500+ institutional strategies on eVestment), so the work is never complete
  • Hours go to reading strategy descriptions and analysing returns, not to meeting managers, where the real insight is
  • Proof of concept: connect structured and unstructured data to an AI committee; the team reviews a challenged recommendation instead of building one

ACIO: owner policy → allocation → ranked, sized manager lineups → Red Team → memo. Code does the math, an information barrier separates roles, every claim cites a tool result, governance rules are enforced in code, and one data gateway can sit over any provider's data.

Seven agents, one committee

React + FastAPI + PydanticAI, OpenAI Responses API (gpt-6-luna), hosted on Render. Code does every calculation; agents cite tool results.

Safety: 17 of 17 defences held

  • Attacks: injection in the ask and in manager documents flagged and wrapped; information barrier; no SQL, file or network tools
  • Malfunctions: uncited facts dropped; off-role tools refused; silent overrides, ignored objections and guardrail breaches stop the run; loops capped
  • Live: hidden instructions planted in 10 manager documents were flagged and not followed
  • Overspending: time, token, dollar and call budgets checked before every call

Live testing: 9 batches, 54 runs

  • The Red Team was ignored, quietly. In 3 of 6 runs the ACIO accepted a major objection about a manager, then gave that manager more money. Fix: the ACIO caps or removes managers, and code refuses any accepted objection that ends with more money in its target
  • The Red Team was reading half the evidence. Tool results were cut at 14,000 characters without notice. Fix: proposals are read one at a time; any cut is marked
  • Edge cases found one batch at a time: mislabelled decisions, a hung run, a Head out of budget, inconsistent lists. Each fixed with a regression test
  • Final batch: 5 recommend with conditions, 1 needs owner input

Lesson: a prompt asking for governance is not governance; the rule has to be checked in code.

Impact

  • First-draft committee package in 2:20 for $0.0727
  • Pilot: today about 2 weeks elapsed for a comparable first draft; with the app 7 minutes to a decision plus 30 minutes of review
  • Pilot user: “It's definitely cool to see the different agents debate and work the problem.”
  • What the pilot could not do: follow the reasoning. Next: a secretary agent that writes minutes a decision-maker can follow
  • Deflated Sharpe reproduces the paper's example (0.9004 / 0.9505)

Limits and next steps

  • Repeat runs of one brief share only half to three quarters of their managers: one run is a draft, not an answer
  • Anonymised sample managers; in-sample mix statistics; no liability model; one model family for all agents
  • Next: expert-scored evaluation set; upload of current policy and holdings; secure sandbox for licensed and private-fund data
  • Then: hedge fund and private markets heads, a pool of Red Team challengers, an independent risk officer, and peer-to-peer consultation between specialist agents

Decision support for a human CIO. Not investment advice.
